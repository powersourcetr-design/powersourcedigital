A store can be well designed, fast, and stocked with good products, and still lose the sale on the last screen because the customer's preferred way to pay is not there. Payments are the one part of an online store where a missing option is not an inconvenience — it is a lost order.

This covers what a Saudi store actually needs and what each method costs. If you are still choosing a platform, [Salla, Zid or Shopify](route:blogPost/salla-vs-zid-vs-shopify) compares how each handles this.

## The methods that matter

**Mada.** The domestic debit network, and the default card for a large share of Saudi shoppers. This is not optional. A checkout offering only international credit cards excludes a significant part of your market at the worst possible moment.

**Visa and Mastercard.** Necessary for international customers and for shoppers who prefer credit.

**Apple Pay.** Disproportionately valuable on mobile, which is where most of your traffic is. It removes the card-entry step entirely, and card entry is where checkouts are abandoned.

**Tabby and Tamara.** Buy now, pay later. Both are widely used in the Kingdom and both lift conversion and basket size on mid to high value orders.

**Cash on delivery.** Still converts in parts of the market, with real costs attached — see below.

## What each costs

Gateway pricing changes and is negotiable at volume, so treat these as shape rather than as quotes. Broadly:

- **Mada** is the cheapest per transaction, which is convenient given it is also the most used
- **International cards** cost more, and more again for foreign-issued cards
- **BNPL providers** charge the most per order — several times a card fee

That last one is worth sitting with. **BNPL is expensive per order and usually still worth it**, because a customer who would not have bought at all is worth more than a fee. But the maths depends on your margin, and it does not hold for low-value baskets.

## The costs people forget

**Refund fees.** Some gateways do not return the original fee on a refund, so a high-return category costs more than the headline rate suggests.

**Settlement delay.** Money reaches your account days after the sale. For a business buying stock against incoming orders, that gap matters more than a fraction of a percent in fees.

**Failed payment recovery.** A meaningful share of failed payments are recoverable if the customer is told clearly what happened. Most stores show a generic error and lose the order.

## What to do at setup

Place one real order through every method you offer, then refund it. It sounds obvious and it is routinely skipped, and the result is a checkout where one option has been quietly failing for a month.

Check the invoice each method produces too — [ZATCA e-invoicing](route:blogPost/zatca-e-invoicing-online-stores) explains what a compliant one needs to contain.

## Getting it set up

Activating gateways requires a commercial registration and a bank account in the business name. Beyond that it is configuration rather than development, and it is included in what we set up on the [e-commerce development](route:landingEcommerce) page — including the test orders, because a gateway that was never tested is a gateway you are trusting on faith.
