In Saudi Arabia, WhatsApp is often not a secondary channel but the first one. The customer who will not call and will not email will message you, and how fast you reply frequently decides whether they buy from you or from whoever replied sooner.

The problem is that most businesses run it from a personal phone with no system behind it. Here is what makes it work.

## Start with the right account

**WhatsApp Business** is free and gives you what the normal app does not: a business profile carrying your address, hours and website link; saved quick replies; an automatic greeting outside working hours; and labels that sort conversations.

Switching takes minutes. Staying on a personal account means every new customer starts from scratch working out who you are.

## Connecting it to your website

A WhatsApp button is among the highest-converting elements on a Saudi business site — provided it is done properly. The link should open a conversation with a prefilled message naming the page the visitor came from.

The difference is practical. A message saying "Hello" means you have to ask what they want. A message saying "I'm asking about e-commerce development" means you already know, and the conversation starts further along.

## Quick replies save hours

Write out, once, the answers to the questions that repeat: rough pricing, timelines, payment terms, what you need to get started. Save them as quick replies.

This is consistency rather than laziness. Every customer gets the same correct information, and the answer no longer depends on who happened to pick up the phone.

## What you must not do

Bulk-sending to numbers that never asked to hear from you gets your number banned. A ban does not cost you a campaign — it costs you the conversation history with your existing customers.

Promotional messaging has a formal route through the Business Platform, requiring prior opt-in and approved templates. Slower, and it does not put your number at risk.

## Measure it, or it teaches you nothing

If WhatsApp is producing customers, you should know which page they came from. The prefilled message solves half of that; click tracking solves the other half.

Without it you will conclude that "WhatsApp brings in customers" while having no idea which page is worth investing in.

## WhatsApp is not a substitute for a website

It is the fastest way to talk to an interested customer, but it does not appear in search results and it does not explain your services to anyone who has not already asked. The website brings you the stranger; WhatsApp converts them.

Businesses that rely on WhatsApp alone tend to find that every new customer came through someone they already knew. If you are weighing where to spend instead, [Google Ads or SEO](route:blogPost/google-ads-vs-seo-saudi) covers that trade-off.
