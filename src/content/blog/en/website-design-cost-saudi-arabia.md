A business website in Saudi Arabia realistically costs between **6,000 and 30,000 SAR**. The range is that wide because the word covers everything from a template with a logo dropped into it to a bilingual site with original copy, custom design and search work behind it.

This explains what separates the bands, what quotes usually leave out, and how to compare two numbers that look nothing alike. Our own scope and pricing are on the [web design](route:landingWebDesign) page if you want the figures without the reasoning.

## What you are actually paying for

Almost the entire cost of a website is people thinking, not people typing. The build is the fastest part. What takes time is deciding what the pages should say, who they are for, and what a visitor is meant to do at the end of them.

That is why a quote with no discovery stage is cheaper: it is not doing that work. Sometimes that is fine, because you may already know exactly what you need. Often it means you receive a good-looking site that says the wrong things.

## The three bands, honestly

**Around 3,000 to 6,000 SAR.** A template, configured. You supply the text and images. The result exists, loads, and carries your logo. For a business that needs an address online this month, this is a sensible purchase and there is no shame in it.

**Around 8,000 to 18,000 SAR.** Structure decided from your actual customers, copy written rather than supplied, a design drawn around your content instead of your content poured into a design, and both languages authored properly. Most Saudi small and mid-size businesses land here.

**Above 20,000 SAR.** Custom functionality, integration with systems you already run, larger page counts, or ongoing search and content work bundled in.

## What quotes leave out

Four things go missing from cheap quotes so consistently that their absence is almost a signature.

- **Content.** "Client to supply text" moves the hardest job to the person least equipped to do it, and is the most common reason a site sits unfinished for months.
- **The Arabic version.** Often quoted as a translation plugin. That is not the same thing, and the difference is obvious to every Arabic-speaking visitor — see [the mistakes Arabic sites make](route:blogPost/arabic-website-design-mistakes).
- **Speed.** Two sites can look identical while one takes four seconds to become usable. That gap costs real enquiries: [what site speed is worth](route:blogPost/website-speed-core-web-vitals).
- **Google Business Profile setup.** For a local business this often produces more enquiries than the website itself. Quoting it separately, or not at all, is common.

## Comparing two quotes properly

Ask both suppliers the same four questions and the numbers usually explain themselves.

1. Who writes the text, and is it included?
2. Is the Arabic version authored, or machine-translated?
3. Who owns the domain, hosting and design files at the end?
4. What happens after launch — is there training, and what does a change cost?

A quote that answers all four confidently is rarely the cheapest, and is usually the one that finishes.

## When not to rebuild

If your current site is structurally sound and the real problem is that it is slow, or your Google profile is unverified, or three key pages have almost nothing on them, a rebuild is an expensive way to fix a cheap problem.
